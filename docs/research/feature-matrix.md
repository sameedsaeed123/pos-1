# Retail POS Feature Matrix and Market Research

This document benchmarks leading global and Pakistani retail point-of-sale solutions, details national compliance and payment rails, and defines the feature matrix for our Pakistani general store point-of-sale system.

## 1. Benchmarked Solutions

### Global Solutions
1. **Square for Retail**: Renowned for rapid touch checkout, barcode creation, smart inventory reordering, and integrated card payments. Lacks Pakistani payment rails, offline synchronization resilience for prolonged power cuts, and dual-language RTL receipts.
2. **Shopify POS**: Excellent omnichannel inventory and clean UI. However, it requires constant reliable connectivity for many core operations, lacks native FBR integration, and pricing is prohibitive for local kiryana stores.
3. **Loyverse POS**: Widely used in small food and retail shops in developing markets. Great offline capability, kitchen display, and basic loyalty. Deficiencies include weak scale barcode support, no native Pakistani udhaar khata ledger, and missing FBR fiscalization.
4. **Lightspeed Retail**: Advanced inventory matrix and purchase order workflow. Overly complex interface for rapid cashier operation in rush hours; poor RTL layout.
5. **Odoo POS**: Strong open-source ERP foundation with offline web worker cache and flexible hardware drivers. Weak default UX for non-technical cashiers; heavy footprint.
6. **Gofrugal**: Dominates South Asian grocery and supermarket retail. Strong batch expiry tracking, loose produce scale barcode reading, and supplier replenishment. Weak modern web UX and legacy Windows client architecture.

### Pakistani Market Solutions
1. **XStak POS**: Leading omnichannel retail point-of-sale in Pakistan deployed in modern marts, apparel, and footwear chains. Includes FBR Tier 1 integration, dual-language receipts, and multi-branch stock sync. Tailored for enterprise rather than fast independent grocery checkout.
2. **DigiKhata / CreditBook / Easy Khata**: Ubiquitous mobile ledgers for Pakistani dukandars. Mastered customer khata (credit book), automatic WhatsApp reminders in Urdu, and customer debt collection. However, they lack barcode checkout, inventory barcode scanning, receipt printers, and cash drawer triggers.

## 2. Regulatory and Payment Standards Research

### FBR Tier 1 Retailers POS Integration
*   **Legal Authority**: Section 40C of Sales Tax Act 1990 and Chapter XIV-AA of Sales Tax Rules 2006.
*   **API Protocol**: RESTful JSON service over HTTPS (`/api/v1/pos/Invoice`).
*   **Payload Requirements**:
    *   `POSID`: 6-digit registered point-of-sale identifier.
    *   `USIN`: Unique Sales Invoice Number generated by POS client.
    *   `DateTime`: ISO timestamp (`YYYY-MM-DD HH:mm:ss`).
    *   `TotalBillAmount`: Total amount payable by consumer.
    *   `TotalQuantity`: Sum of item quantities.
    *   `TotalSaleValue`: Net taxable value before tax.
    *   `TotalTaxCharged`: General Sales Tax (GST) amount.
    *   `PaymentMode`: 1 = Cash, 2 = Card, 3 = Mobile Wallet / QR, 4 = Credit (Khata).
    *   `InvoiceType`: 1 = Standard Sale, 2 = Return / Debit Note.
    *   `Items`: Array of item code, item name, quantity, total amount, sale value, tax charged, and PCT code.
*   **FBR Verification & Receipt Output**:
    *   FBR returns a 19-digit fiscal invoice number (example: `100101-151126-0001-9`).
    *   Receipt must print this FBR Invoice Number along with an FBR verification QR code containing invoice verification URL and summary string.
*   **Offline Mode Mandate**: In case of power or internet disruption, checkout must never halt. Invoices queue into local encrypted storage with sequential offline identifiers and sync automatically in batch once internet returns, updating the printed receipt status.

### Pakistani Payment Rails
1. **Raast (State Bank of Pakistan Instant Payment)**: Standardized EMVCo QR code payload enabling zero-fee account-to-account transfers. The POS displays a dynamic or static Raast QR, allowing customers to scan with any banking app. The cashier logs the transaction reference number.
2. **JazzCash & Easypaisa**: Dominant mobile wallets with over 35 million active wallets. Retail stores utilize merchant till codes and QR displays. Cashier captures customer mobile or transaction ID for audit tracking.
3. **Cash in PKR**: Cash remains king in Pakistani grocery stores. Pakistani Rupee notes (Rs 10, 20, 50, 75, 100, 500, 1000, 5000) require dedicated one-tap tender shortcuts and instant change computation.
4. **Udhaar (Khata Ledger)**: Core to neighbourhood kiryana trust. Customers purchase groceries on monthly credit. A production-grade POS must treat Udhaar as a first-class payment method with hard credit limits, balance ledgers, and one-click WhatsApp balance statements.

### Barcode & Hardware Standards
*   **Keystroke Dynamics**: Hardware USB / Bluetooth handheld scanners emulate an HID keyboard. Characters arrive within 20 to 35 milliseconds of each other, ending with `Enter` (`\r` or `\n`). Manual typing by human cashiers averages 100 to 250 milliseconds per character. Global window event listeners using timestamp deltas detect hardware scans reliably without requiring an active input focus.
*   **Barcode Formats**: EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A, and Code-128. EAN-13 check digit validation prevents corrupt reads.
*   **Weighing Scale In-Store Barcodes**: Prefix 20 to 29 (in-store retail usage per GS1). A standard 13-digit scale barcode follows `20 AAAAA WWWWW C` where `20` is the price/weight prefix, `AAAAA` is the 5-digit product SKU, `WWWWW` is weight in grams or price in paisa, and `C` is the checksum.
*   **Thermal Printers**: Standard 80mm (48 characters per line) and 58mm (32 characters per line) thermal printers using ESC/POS protocol (`ESC @` reset, `ESC p` cash drawer pulse, `GS V` paper cut). For Urdu receipts, thermal bitmap raster printing (`GS v 0`) is used to bypass hardware font ROM limitations.
